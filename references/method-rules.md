# RSA copy method

The judgment rules this skill applies. They are the public edition of TNT Growth's approved Responsive Search Ad method (Google Ads method release `2026-10-14.1`, written by Adam Treboutat). Platform facts link to Google's documentation and were checked in October 2026; Google changes the platform often, so re-verify any fact a decision depends on.

## What good copy is for

Write ads that make the offer clear, attract the right buyer and turn relevant searches into valuable customers. The deliverable is the actual headlines, descriptions, destinations and pinning plan for each ad group, not advice for someone else to write them.

Judge the result by customer quality, acquisition cost, volume and value. Ad Strength can point to omissions; it is not the performance score. In Adam Treboutat's published analysis of 3,900 Google ads across 65 accounts, Ad Strength had essentially no correlation with within-account CPA rank (r = 0.002) ([the published study](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7490761101337350144/)). That is an observational result from those accounts, not a universal causal law.

This method writes and improves Search RSAs inside the campaign structure you already have. It can flag a structural or landing-page problem and keep going on the copy; it does not rebuild campaigns, change matching or switch conversion goals.

## 1. Read the account and the real offer

Confirm the campaigns and ad groups in scope. Read every enabled RSA in that scope, plus relevant paused ads, prior versions and existing winners. Collect keyword themes, observed searches, text, pins, ad status, policy issues, destinations and Ad Strength suggestions. If you only have a dated export, use it and state the gaps.

Inspect the effective destination beyond the ad's URL: keyword and mobile overrides, tracking, dynamic URL settings and relevant assets. Include campaign-level text assets, text disclaimer assets and Google-generated text that can change the message. A recent performance report is not a substitute for the configuration.

Read the product, pricing, eligibility, differentiators, proof, brand style and landing pages. Establish what the business can actually deliver. Carry forward the agreed conversion definition and mature customer economics; do not assume a cheap signup is bad or an expensive lead is good.

Diagnose concrete problems: an unclear offer, unsupported claims, the wrong buyer, missing qualifications, repetitive assets, incompatible combinations, weak destinations or a broken next step. Do not declare an ad group unsalvageable from its keyword count, and do not require a full account audit because a few cosmetic checks fail.

## 2. Define the buying decision before drafting

For each ad group, state the intended need, the offer, why it fits, what makes it credible and the next step. Name the objections and qualifications that change whether someone should click.

Use truthful buyer filters where they help: a real minimum price, a team-size requirement, a service area, a product limitation or an eligibility condition. Keep the distinction clear. "Designed for enterprise teams" describes positioning; "Minimum 50 users" states a requirement. Do not turn an attractive customer profile into a restriction the business does not have.

A qualifier can reduce clicks or form fills while improving customer economics. Written badly, it can also exclude worthwhile buyers. Choose the filter deliberately, then judge mature customer cost, quality and volume rather than CTR alone.

Anchor copy to the need instead of repeating every keyword. Website creation, landing-page creation and A/B testing can need different promises and proof. Claims about speed or conversion lifts need evidence; a product category does not supply it.

Shared copy is valid when it serves the searches well. Competitor keywords can share one ad group with keyword-level final URLs pointing to the relevant comparison pages ([Google keyword destinations](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6371202?hl=en)). Separate groups remain an option when the copy or management needs differ. Do not manufacture differences just to make every ad unique.

Write brand copy for the branded task the campaign should serve. Acquisition, pricing, login and support are different needs. Competitor copy must make the advertiser's identity and offer clear. Do not imply affiliation or repeat a competitor's claim as the advertiser's own. When one ad group serves several competitors, keep competitor names out of the shared copy (a headline naming one would show to people searching another); name a competitor only in an ad group dedicated to it, and only with substantiation and a policy check.

## 3. Write useful assets within the real limits

| Field | Creation requirement or limit |
|---|---|
| Headlines | 3 to 15; up to 30 characters each |
| Descriptions | 2 to 4; up to 90 characters each |
| Display paths | Optional; up to 15 characters per field |

Count spaces and punctuation. Double-width characters count twice. Validate final text with the platform's rules, not a word-processor count ([Google RSA specifications](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791?hl=en)). These are field limits, not a reason to stop early.

Normally fill all 15 headline and four description slots with useful, truthful variation. Work through the product or problem, meaningful capabilities, outcomes, supportable proof, qualifiers, objection answers and relevant calls to action. For a well-understood offer it should not be hard to find enough angles. If a first pass stops at ten headlines, keep going before calling it complete.

When verified facts look thin, the answer is more angles, not more claims: the buyer's problem in their words, who it is for, use cases, objections answered with facts you already have, the qualifier, and distinct calls to action the page supports. Restating one fact two ways is padding; addressing a different buyer question with the same fact is not.

Use fewer only for a specific, stated reason, such as a deliberately narrow message set or account evidence for that choice. Never fill a slot with invented proof or repetition. The technical minimum is not the writing target, and an underdeveloped draft is not a justified exception.

There is no fixed allocation (no "6 keyword headlines, 7 benefits, 3 CTAs"), no short-headline quota, no mandatory keyword insertion, no location-insertion quota and no first-word rule. Vary openings and emphasis; avoid a wall of brand-name openings in non-brand ads. Keep real brand spellings and legitimate acronyms. Title case is a style choice; gimmicky capitalization can violate policy ([Google capitalization policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14848295?hl=en)).

Make each asset understandable on its own and coherent with every permitted companion. Avoid fragments that need another asset, repeated CTAs, contradictory prices, incompatible audiences, and claims whose essential condition may not show. Check whether a broad "Free" claim misrepresents a time-limited trial when the qualification is absent.

Descriptions should add useful detail: the offer, evidence, an objection answer or the action to take. Match the CTA to the real experience. "Start Free" must reach an available free start; "Book a Demo" must not land on an unrelated contact page.

Write for flexible rendering. Headlines can appear in description or link positions, and eligible assets can be borrowed from another active RSA in the same ad group, linking to that RSA's destination. Review compatibility across the whole group, including those pages. A keyword-level URL override does not make every clickable asset follow the keyword's page ([Google asset flexibility](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111?hl=en)).

Dynamic features are optional. If keyword insertion is used, validate its rendered options and fallback; it inserts the matched keyword, not necessarily the literal query, and is never worth enabling just to raise a score ([Google keyword insertion](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6371157?hl=en)). Keep keyword insertion out of competitor, insurance-carrier and other trademark ad groups, where it can put another company's name in your headline and draw trademark complaints. Location insertion and "near you" claims must match where the service is actually delivered; for a fixed facility, the searcher's city can imply a local presence the business does not have.

Any deliberate comparison with a competitor needs real substantiation and a current policy check. Do not invent a universal legal ban to explain why you avoided one.

## 4. Pin only for a named reason

Default to unpinned assets when the message stays accurate in every allowed combination. Pin when consistent visibility is necessary for an approved brand requirement, a required disclosure or a truthful commercial qualifier the business deliberately wants every searcher to see. Name the message each pin protects; "message control" in general is not a reason.

If a pinned asset shows the wrong message to some of the group's searchers, unpin it rather than pinning a replacement. Pinning Headline 1 and Headline 2 leaves unpinned headlines eligible only for Headline 3, which does not always show, so treat dual pinning as an exception with a stated reason.

Pinning limits combinations. It is a tradeoff, not proof of worse acquisition or higher CPC. Do not remove a useful filter only to improve Ad Strength.

Several distinct alternatives may share one pinned position; that is supported. Every option in a required position must carry the required message. Google gives Description 1 as its disclaimer example; Headline 3 and Description 2 are not dependable positions for always-required text ([Google pinning guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791?hl=en)).

Check landing-page automation before depending on a pin. Google's documented configuration for retaining pins disables Final URL expansion and URL inclusions. Resolve that specific conflict; it does not require turning off every AI Max feature ([Google dynamic URLs and pins](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15909989?hl=en)).

Check campaign text disclaimer assets too. An approved disclaimer replaces pinned Description 1, so resolve any conflict with a required qualifier there. Where available, these assets offer a disclosure route compatible with AI Max, including Final URL expansion. They are not an unconditional guarantee: rare truncation is possible, and removal or disapproval can leave the RSA serving without the disclaimer. Check approval, coverage and the fallback, and decide what happens if the required text is lost ([Google text disclaimers](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17028951?hl=en)). Never delete a required disclosure just to restore a displaced qualifier. When the only workable fix is in the disclaimer asset itself (for example, revising it to carry both messages), hand that to whoever owns the asset and compliance sign-off, keep the pin as a fallback, and hold activation of the affected ads until it is resolved. Check the disclaimer field's current length limit in Google's documentation rather than assuming it.

## 5. Check the complete ad and deliver the copy

Know which statements need evidence. Objective claims do: numbers, prices, ratings, guarantees, speed or outcome promises, comparisons and superlatives. Plain descriptions of what the business offers (its features, service area, how to apply, the next step) can come from the business's own brief or site; record that as the source. A product description is not a performance claim, so do not strip ordinary copy like "Apply Online" or "Fixed Monthly Payments" when the business states it.

Verify each objective claim against its source and the destination. Record the source, scope and qualification for numbers, prices, guarantees, comparisons and superlatives. Someone confirming a claim identifies what they want to say; it does not create substantiation. Do not present an exceptional result as typical. Keep unsupported claims out of launch copy and ask for the missing evidence in one message ([Google unreliable-claims policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857?hl=en)). In regulated categories, also check the jurisdiction's advertising rules, such as limits on promoting gambling bonuses or required health disclosures. A true claim can still be one the ad may not make.

Check spelling, counts, duplication, offer consistency, every allowed combination, pins, destinations, mobile behavior and the actual conversion path. A preview helps but does not show every combination or prove routing.

Read Ad Strength's specific suggestions. It is not a serving requirement or an input to Ad Rank or Quality Score. Its diagnosis includes keyword relevance, combinations and sitelinks. "Average" does not mean an ad is missing headlines or performing badly. Fix useful omissions; do not remove qualifications, invent assets or enable automation only to raise the grade ([Google Ad Strength](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843?hl=en)).

Normally prepare two meaningfully different RSAs per ad group when the offer supports useful variation. Google's current guidance recommends at least two, each with a unique final URL. Treat that as guidance: use the page that fits, and do not manufacture a second destination or a near-duplicate ad to meet a count. Up to three RSAs can be enabled per ad group, and existing enabled ads count toward that limit ([Google account limits](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372658?hl=en)).

For each proposed RSA, deliver:

- Campaign and ad-group identity, intended buyer and copy angle.
- Every headline and description with its character count, plus display paths.
- Pins, their positions, the message each protects and permitted alternatives.
- Ad final URL, any keyword or mobile destinations, and preserved tracking.
- Claim evidence, required qualifications, dynamic settings and unresolved dependencies.
- Whether it creates a new RSA, edits an existing one or keeps one, what stays active, and the review question.

Keep this review version separate from the clean import file.

## 6. Publish within scope and judge real outcomes

Preserve the prior text, pins, URLs and dates. Create new ads paused so the configuration can be checked, then enable them. Keep a working eligible ad live while a replacement is reviewed.

An edit is not always a new ad. Google keeps version history, and the API can update an existing RSA's ad ID. After any change, read back the actual text, pins, URLs, status and policy result, and reconcile partial failures before retrying ([Google version history](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7502216?hl=en), [RSA updates](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/ads/mutate-ads)).

Soon after launch, inspect delivery, unwanted queries, destination behavior and generated text. Judge the business result after the conversion lag. Two weeks can be a checkpoint, but time or impressions alone do not establish a winning ad. Keep unrelated goals, bids and pages stable during a copy comparison, and note any change you had to make.

Use asset statistics for diagnosis. Google's UI has deprecated the old Performance label column; labels from an export or connector are context, not instructions. Asset rows describe assets serving together: do not sum them into account totals or treat an asset's CPA as its isolated effect ([Google asset report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9564897?hl=en)). A frequently served combination is not a randomized winner you can freeze into a fixed ad with the same result ([Google combinations report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11894820?hl=en)).

Two RSAs in one ad group are not equally allocated test arms. If a causal answer matters, run a supported experiment; otherwise report the comparison as observational. Keep required truthful messages and working winners, improve specific weaknesses for a stated reason, then judge mature customer economics again.

## Worked examples (hypothetical, not client results)

1. **A real price floor should filter buyers.** A product starts at $500/month, but many inquiries expect something much cheaper. Write "Plans Start At $500/Month," supported on the page. Pin it if consistent visibility matters, using a setup that retains pins. Judge customer economics and volume, not Ad Strength.
2. **Finish the useful set.** An RSA has ten truthful headlines, three descriptions, acceptable mature economics and Average Ad Strength. Proposed additions repeat existing points or claim an unsupported "10x faster." Reject those and keep writing truthful alternatives toward 15 and 4. Do not fabricate proof to finish the count or disrupt a working ad to raise its grade.
3. **Pinned text gets replaced.** A required qualifier is pinned to Description 1, and an approved campaign disclaimer replaces that position. Resolve both requirements with a supported setup. Do not assume the pin protects the qualifier, and do not remove the disclaimer to restore it.
